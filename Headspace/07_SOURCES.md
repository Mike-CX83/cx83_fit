# Sources and evidence register

Reviewed November 10, 2026. Links point to original research, instrument owners, official platform documentation, or original software repositories. These sources support individual design choices; none validates the proposed combined Headspace formula.

## Research and instruments

### R01

**Brief Addiction Monitor.** [VA clinical overview](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/healthcare-providers/sud.asp), [VA instructions and scoring cautions](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/healthcare-providers/docs/VA_OMH_The_Brief_Addiction-Monitor_Instuctions_508.pdf), and [original development study, Cacciola and colleagues, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22898042/).

Supports separating use, risk, and protective factors. The current VA guidance cautions against treating the instrument as a psychometrically refined overall total. [A later factor-structure study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29866381/) also provides reasons for caution with subscales. Instrument use and version must be reviewed before embedding.

### R02

**AWARE.** [Original prospective validation, Miller and Harris, 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11022817/), [young-adult validation, Kelly and colleagues, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21700396/), [revised questionnaire and instructions](https://cosap.unm.edu/assets/inst/aware.pdf), and [CASAA assessment library terms](https://casaa.unm.edu/tools/assessment-instruments.html).

Supports warning-sign assessment in specified treatment populations. Revised form has 28 items. The published probabilities are population and outcome specific, not a universal daily estimate. CASAA describes these tools as free to use and intended for professional use. The hypothesized fixed sequence of warning signs was not established by the original study.

### R03

**Repeated brief assessments and recurrence.** [Using ecological momentary assessments to predict relapse after adult substance use treatment, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29499393/).

An exploratory study using 21,897 observations from 43 adults. Supports investigation of current/recent use, craving, and negative affect, with careful attention to time horizon. Does not validate a text-only journal score or broad consumer risk percentage.

### R04

**Temporally precise alcohol lapse prediction.** [Wyant and colleagues, 2024, full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11556439/).

Used 151 people in early alcohol recovery, repeated EMA, and distinct prediction horizons. Useful for outcome definitions, longitudinal features, and evaluation design. More frequent reporting, treatment context, and cohort characteristics limit direct transfer to a once-daily consumer check-in.

### R05

**Longer warning windows.** [Wyant and colleagues, PLOS One, September 8, 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0356893).

Studies delayed prediction windows using repeated EMA from an early alcohol recovery cohort. Reported performance varied by subgroup. The paper links [OSF study materials](https://osf.io/xta67/); that page returned HTTP 403 in this review, so scripts, data permissions, and model artifacts were not independently inspected. Treat these as a follow-up lead.

### R06

**PACS.** [Flannery, Volpicelli, and Pettinati, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10470970/).

Original study of the five-item Penn Alcohol Craving Scale, concerning the previous week, in 147 participants from a treatment trial. Supports a focused craving measure, not conversion into a universal daily score. Exact redistribution terms for an embedded commercial questionnaire remain to be confirmed.

### R07

**Recovery capital.** [BARC-10 development and validation, Vilsaint and colleagues, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28578224/), [instrument from the Recovery Research Institute](https://www.recoveryanswers.org/assets/barc10.pdf), and [NIH instrument listing](https://www.nih.gov/node/21021).

Measures recovery resources and strengths. Validation concerns recovery capital and sustained remission, not a calibrated prediction of the next episode. NIH lists copyright as no; preserve attribution and verify the version used.

### R08

**Oura, EMA, and recovery support.** [Identifying risk factors for drug use recurrence with ecological momentary assessment, wearable technologies, and machine learning, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42490870/).

A feasibility trial with 229 enrolled, substantial attrition and missing data, and a smaller randomized comparison. Supports feasibility research and the importance of adherence. Does not establish a general consumer relapse detector or the effectiveness of this proposed Headspace system.

### R09

**Gambling.** [Hawker and colleagues, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32759020/).

EMA study of 97 regular gamblers and 5,113 observations examining cravings, self-efficacy, and gambling behavior. Supports researching these constructs in a gambling-specific module, not reusing alcohol coefficients.

### R10

**Compulsive sexual behavior measures.** [CSBD-19 development, Bothe and colleagues, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32609629/) and [CSBD-DI development, Grubbs and colleagues, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36913189/).

Screening and construct-validation studies. Neither supplies a validated daily relapse algorithm for this app. Assess impairment and impaired control in context; do not use sexual frequency or moral disapproval as substitutes. Exact questionnaire reuse terms require version-specific review.

### R11

**Elevated resting heart rate.** [American Heart Association, Tachycardia](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/arrhythmia/about-arrhythmia/tachycardia--fast-heart-rate).

Supports the distinction between a potentially important cardiovascular finding and its many possible causes. A wearable heart-rate change cannot establish drinking or drug use. This is a design example, not an assessment of the owner's current heart rate.

### R12

**Intended use and product claims.** [FDA General Wellness guidance, January 2026](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices), [guidance text](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download), and [FDA examples of software functions under enforcement discretion](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-which-fda-will-exercise-enforcement-discretion).

Product functionality, claims, and intended use matter. This research does not determine regulatory classification. A clinical prediction or monitoring product requires a specific assessment; adding wellness wording is not that assessment.

### R13

**WHO-5.** [WHO publication](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/who-ucn-msd-mhe-2024.01) and [English instrument PDF](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-5_english-original4da539d6ed4b49389e3afe47cda2326a.pdf).

Five wellbeing items with a two-week recall period. The reviewed WHO PDF states CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO. Confirm the exact edition and permissions before commercial distribution or adaptation.

### R14

**PHQ and GAD screeners.** [Publisher portal](https://www.phqscreeners.com/) and [University of Washington AIMS Center PHQ-9 resource](https://aims.uw.edu/resource/phq-9-depression-scale/).

The AIMS resource states that PHQ-9 reproduction, translation, display, and distribution do not require permission. The portal's current extracted page did not expose every instrument's terms. Confirm GAD-7 and translation details before embedding. These are symptom measures, not a daily capacity or relapse probability.

### R15

**PROMIS.** [HealthMeasures obtain and administer guidance](https://www.healthmeasures.net/explore-measurement-systems/promis/obtain-administer-measures).

Electronic administration can require HealthMeasures Electronic Administration Permission and review, with specific exemptions. Free paper access does not by itself settle product integration rights.

### R16

**Cycle context.** [Oura study in 26 healthy participants, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35422659/) and [young and midlife Oura study, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39108015/).

Supports considering cycle-associated physiological variation. The findings do not establish a standard mental health deduction or justify inferring hormone levels. Individual circumstances, study population, and measurement timing matter.

### R17

**Language model transfer limits.** [Psychosis relapse prediction with clinical notes, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35479497/).

Illustrates a different outcome, text type, and population, with a drop between internal and external validation performance. It is not a recommended model for personal journals or addiction recovery.

### R18

**Wearable predictors and confounders.** [Week-Ahead Prediction of High-Risk Drinking Episodes Among Young Adults, 2026](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e88223).

Prospective observational modeling of future high-risk drinking. Its discussion distinguishes predictive correlates from causes and notes contextual confounders. Does not validate direct nutritional or physiological penalties in Headspace.

### R19

**Incremental value of passive sensing.** [Cellular communication sensing and lapse prediction in early AUD recovery, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42627809/).

Study of 144 people; the combined model did not demonstrate clear incremental discrimination over baseline features. Supports requiring added value before increasing data collection or surveillance.

## Platform documentation

### P01

**Oura API.** [Official V2 reference](https://cloud.ouraring.com/v2/docs) and [official authentication documentation](https://cloud.ouraring.com/docs/authentication).

The dynamic V2 page did not expose readable schema text through this research tool. The repository's [earlier schema review](../docs/OURA_REUSE_REVIEW.md) and the current standalone collection catalog were also inspected. The known schema URL could not be fetched in this session. Recheck the current schema and actual authorized responses before implementing new fields or scopes. The successful account check establishes provider response, not dashboard ingestion.

### P02

**Oura physiological stress.** [Official Daytime Stress documentation](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/21205822135315-Daytime-Stress).

Explains personal physiological baselines, contributing signals, and several possible stressors. App features and API field availability must be verified separately.

### P03

**HealthKit and sleep samples.** [HealthKit overview](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/) and [sleep analysis categories](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis).

Supports permissioned native access and the interpretation of overlapping sleep sample types. Not proof of server access or a browser HealthKit API.

### P04

**Apple Sleep Score availability.** [Apple support explanation](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/108906) and [Apple Developer Technical Support answer about API access](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/800403).

The feature exists in Apple's user interface. The reviewed DTS answer says there was no API at the time of that answer. Treat access as unverified in the implementation target until the current SDK confirms it. Do not assert permanent unavailability based only on an older forum answer.

### P05

**Clinical HealthKit records.** [Apple accessing health records](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/accessing-health-records).

Describes clinical records represented as permissioned FHIR resources, with additional setup requirements. Availability depends on what supported institutions supply.

### P06

**Apple HRV method.** [HealthKit SDNN documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/heartratevariabilitysdnn).

Supports keeping Apple's SDNN separate from RMSSD and preserving the measurement method and window.

### P07

**Cycle records.** [HealthKit menstrual flow](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier/menstrualflow).

Documents period samples and cycle-start metadata. Permissioned records are not inferred pregnancy or hormone measurements.

### P08

**MyFitnessPal API access.** [Official developer portal](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/id/api.lz).

Describes an API available to approved developers. Does not establish that a personal connector has product-wide rights or stable public API access.

### P09

**MyChart and Epic FHIR.** [Epic patient-facing application documentation](https://fhir.epic.com/Documentation?docId=patientfacingfhirapps&section=AutomaticClientDistribution) and [Epic SMART support FAQ](https://vendorservices.epic.com/FAQ/Index?Question=3106).

Supports patient authorization, provider-specific endpoints, and resource-scoped access. Does not promise every historical record type or automatic availability from a ChatGPT connection.

### P10

**Apple wellbeing APIs.** [WWDC24 wellbeing APIs](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10109/).

Describes State of Mind and anxiety/depression assessment APIs. These are distinct data types; do not silently convert them into the existing mood rating or copy them as independent corroboration of the same report.

### P11

**HealthKit privacy and empty reads.** [Apple documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy.md).

Explains separate permission by type, the inability to distinguish denied read permission from absent data, and device-lock limitations for background reads. These limits shape missingness and synchronization behavior.

## Original software repositories

License and push-date observations are summarized in [05_OPEN_SOURCE.md](05_OPEN_SOURCE.md). Repository API metadata was checked on the review date. A recent push is not a security audit or production endorsement.

### O01

[medspaCy](https://github.com/medspacy/medspacy). MIT. Modular clinical NLP and context handling. Not a journal scoring or addiction prediction system.

### O02

[ruptures](https://github.com/deepcharles/ruptures). BSD 2 clause. Offline statistical change point detection. Not a clinical interpretation layer.

### O03

[River](https://github.com/online-ml/river). BSD 3 clause. Streaming statistics and machine learning. Any adaptation of decision thresholds still needs explicit control.

### O04

[fhir.resources](https://github.com/nazrulworld/fhir.resources), [README](https://github.com/nazrulworld/fhir.resources/blob/main/README.rst), and [license](https://github.com/nazrulworld/fhir.resources/blob/main/LICENSE). The actual license has three BSD conditions despite repository metadata returning NOASSERTION. Validate the exact supported FHIR version against source records.

### O05

[SpeziHealthKit](https://github.com/StanfordSpezi/SpeziHealthKit). MIT. Native HealthKit collection tools. A possible companion-app component, not a direct web integration.

### O06

[NeuroKit2](https://github.com/neuropsychology/NeuroKit). MIT. Raw physiological signal processing. It cannot reconstruct raw beat intervals from a daily summary.

### O07

[RADAR Questionnaire](https://github.com/RADAR-base/RADAR-Questionnaire). Apache 2.0. Questionnaire delivery and monitoring platform component. Software license does not license every questionnaire delivered through it.

### O08

[LAMP Cortex](https://github.com/BIDMCDigitalPsychiatry/LAMP-cortex), BSD 3 clause, and [LAMP dashboard](https://github.com/BIDMCDigitalPsychiatry/LAMP-dashboard), AGPL 3.0 only as specified in [its license](https://github.com/BIDMCDigitalPsychiatry/LAMP-dashboard/blob/master/LICENSE.md). Do not assume one license across the platform.

### O09

[RAPIDS](https://github.com/carissalow/rapids). AGPL 3.0. Reproducible feature extraction from mobile and wearable streams. Broader collection and operational needs are outside the pilot.

### O10

[VADER](https://github.com/cjhutto/vaderSentiment). MIT. Sentiment processing developed for language valence; not a measure of recovery, truthfulness, or clinical risk.

### O11

[TS4H 4Hz, 4 Pages](https://github.com/AbhishekSinghDhadwal/TS4H_4Hz_4Pages). Apache 2.0. Research pipeline with high-frequency physiological channels. The README's datasets include WESAD, exam stress, and CAN-STRESS; an addiction-oriented title does not change the observed training targets.

### O12

[HRV AUD calibration](https://github.com/Om-C2026/hrv-aud-calibration). MIT. Stress classification and device-comparison research. Repository limitations identify proxy outcomes and a single-person ambulatory comparison. No production model accepted.

### O13

[NLML](https://github.com/nicolasleenaerts/NLML). EMA modeling scripts in R. GitHub metadata did not establish a license. No code copied, installed, or cleared for reuse.

## Repository evidence and review limits

The private local source and its current roadmap, scoring modules, journal handoff, verification record, and latest coordination handoff were read. Older verification entries describe historical checkpoints; they were not rerun here. Existing provider originals and clinical records were not opened for this planning task.

Newly checked tool behavior: Oura account self-check returned a provider response; the Oura collection catalog returned available interfaces; MyFitnessPal's configuration status explicitly did not verify a live account. These checks expose no personal measurements in this package. Health and Apple record transfer remain untested.

Research limitations: some dynamic documentation did not render; the current Oura schema URL was not retrievable; OSF materials returned an access error; no dependency or model was executed; no clinical validation dataset was acquired. The recommendations distinguish those gaps from positive findings.

Before implementation, refresh time-sensitive API and license facts at a pinned version. Before deployment, test the actual authorized path. Before public clinical claims, obtain evidence appropriate to that claim.
